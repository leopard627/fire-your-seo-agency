# Content Quality — QRG and E-E-A-T Audit

Check **whether a page serves its reader's purpose and offers evidence readers can trust**.
This practical audit draws on Google's public guidance; it does not implement the entire QRG process.
E-E-A-T is not a single ranking factor. Do not offer an official E-E-A-T score or ranking guarantee.
For audit-only requests, deliver findings and recommendations. Edit, publish, or delete only within the requested scope.

## 1. Inputs and sampling

- Establish the target URLs, audience, language/region, and page purpose. Reuse context instead of asking again.
- Use a sitemap, URL inventory, or Search Console page/query data when available.
  Without access, proceed with public pages and leave traffic, conversion, and ranking causes unverified.
- For large sites, sample the homepage, key service/product pages, informational articles, and author/about pages.
  Include recent and older articles, plus confirmed declining pages when available.
- Record sampled URLs, selection reasons, and audit date. Do not describe a sample as a complete site audit.

## 2. Purpose and reader value

- [ ] State **who visits and which question or task they need to resolve** for each page.
- [ ] Does the body fulfill the title's promise? Are conditions, steps, and exceptions sufficient for the next action?
- [ ] Do ads, repeated copy, or unnecessary introductions obstruct the main answer?
- [ ] Are claims and examples specific? Is there useful information beyond retelling other articles?

Pair each missing element with the actual passage and the question left unanswered.
Example: "The audience is listed, but required prior experience is missing, so beginners cannot judge eligibility."
Do not make length a goal. A short page that fulfills its purpose need not be expanded.

## 3. Experience, expertise, authoritativeness, and trust

Judge evidence against the topic and risk. Personal accounts and professional advice need different qualifications.
For YMYL advice affecting health, finances, or safety, pay particular attention to suitable evidence and review.

| Element | Evidence to examine | Distinguish in findings |
|---|---|---|
| Experience | First-hand process, conditions, results, original photos/records | No experience vs. no evidence found on the page |
| Expertise | Relevant work, qualifications, reviewer, claims and sources | A listed credential vs. verification of a particular claim |
| Authoritativeness | Independent coverage, citations, and reputation in the field | Self-promotion vs. independent sources; namesakes vs. the actual entity |
| Trust | Responsible party, contact details, accuracy, sourcing, interests | Confirmed error vs. insufficient information to judge |

- [ ] Cross-check selected key claims with original sources and verify that those sources support the claims.
- [ ] Is author/operator information connected to the content? Never invent identities or qualifications.
- [ ] For purchase/booking pages, are pricing conditions, contact, and cancellation/refund information sufficient?
- [ ] For product evaluations, are relevant sponsorship/provision interests and evaluation methods explained?
- [ ] When researching reputation, record queries, sources, and dates. Missing coverage is not a negative reputation.

Do not create unsupported credentials, customer testimonials, or experience photos to fill trust gaps.
Separate "real evidence the operator must supply" from "copy supportable with current evidence."

## 4. Duplicate, thin, and outdated content

- **Duplicate candidates**: group URLs answering the same question and compare unique examples, audiences, and purposes.
  Shared boilerplate or topics alone do not establish duplication or a search penalty.
- **Thin answers**: record unresolved questions and missing evidence rather than relying on word counts.
  Low impressions or traffic alone do not establish poor content quality.
- **Outdated information**: compare prices, schedules, rules, and tool guidance with verifiable current sources.
  An old publication date alone is not an error; do not change dates without substantive updates.
- **Similar pages at scale**: when only place/product names differ, examine their additional value for each audience.
  Do not use AI involvement or detector scores as a content-quality verdict.

Recommend keeping, strengthening evidence, updating, or considering consolidation for each page.
Treat consolidation, deletion, and noindex as proposals informed by reader purpose, unique content, and available traffic/link data.
Do not execute them or clean up a whole site in response to an audit-only request.

## 5. Reporting

Lead with the main findings and priorities, then provide a URL-level table.
Mark uninspected pages and inaccessible evidence **unverified**.

| Target URL | Finding | Evidence URL and passage/observation | Impact and priority | Specific improvement | Uncertainty/additional input |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Inspected URL | Observed issue | Reproducible evidence | Reader's practical difficulty | Location and material needed | What could not be verified and why |

- Prioritize confirmed factual errors and misleading conditions, followed by missing answers and supporting evidence.
- Briefly record reusable strengths from good pages.
- When edits are also requested, retain before/after details and verified sources. Never fabricate missing evidence.
- State sample size, audit date, inputs, and unknowns; do not claim a causal explanation for ranking changes.
